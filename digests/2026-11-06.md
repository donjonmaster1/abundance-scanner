## Effective Service Provision

### Cost Standards and Procedures; Purchasing and Property Management
- **Jurisdiction:** US Federal — Legal Services Corporation
- **Source:** [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/11/05/2026-22649/cost-standards-and-procedures-purchasing-and-property-management)
- **Status:** Rule (as of 2026-11-05)
- **Summary:** The Legal Services Corporation finalized a rule that raises the dollar threshold requiring prior approval for recipient spending, clarifies documentation and fundraising-proceeds rules, and gives LSC more flexibility in reviewing prior-approval requests from legal aid grantees. The change reduces administrative paperwork burdens on the nonprofit organizations that use federal funds to provide civil legal aid to low-income Americans, while aiming to keep accountability intact.
- **Why it matters:** A modest but real administrative efficiency gain for the federal legal aid system, freeing up staff time at LSC-funded legal aid organizations to focus on client services rather than compliance paperwork.

## Housing Supply

### Council Variance #CV23-084, 1462-1466 HAMLET ST. (43201)
- **Jurisdiction:** Columbus, OH
- **Source:** [Legistar (municipal)](https://columbus.legistar.com/LegislationDetail.aspx?ID=129295&GUID=6106DFDC-66A8-4F9E-AA32-1A6A86B6EBA1)
- **Status:** Passed (as of 2026-11-03)
- **Summary:** Columbus City Council approved a variance for the property at 1462-1466 Hamlet St. in the Weinland Park/University District area, waiving strict R-4 zoning rules on parking, lot coverage, floor area ratio, lot width, and yard setbacks. The variance allows construction of two single-unit dwellings on each lot instead of one, effectively doubling housing capacity on the site. The council passed it as an emergency measure, allowing immediate effect.
- **Why it matters:** A small but concrete example of local zoning flexibility enabling incremental infill housing density in an urban Columbus neighborhood near Ohio State University.
